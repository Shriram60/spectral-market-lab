51 NSE stocks, 2021 to 2025

Most of the stock market is noise.

A linear algebra library written from scratch, pointed at the Indian equity market. Of the 51 eigenvalues of the stocks' correlation matrix, only five rise above what pure randomness would produce. This page shows what that structure is good for, and where it fails.

Ten questions, answered out of sample

Every method is scored on data it never saw, against a simple baseline, with a significance test. Where it doesn't beat the baseline, that's the answer.

    Numerics

    Before the market, the machinery. The same maths can be computed in ways that differ by fifteen orders of magnitude in accuracy.

    Does the choice of QR algorithm matter?

    Three ways to orthogonalise the same matrix. As the matrix gets closer to singular, classical Gram-Schmidt loses orthogonality as ε·κ², modified Gram-Schmidt as ε·κ, and Householder reflections not at all.

    How it's measured

    Matrices with prescribed condition numbers from 1 to 10¹³ are built from random orthogonal factors. Each algorithm's Q is checked with ‖I − QᵀQ‖₂, which is zero for a perfectly orthogonal matrix.

    Is the normal-equations shortcut safe?

    Fitting a trend through day numbers 1, 2, 3… builds a badly conditioned matrix. The normal equations square that condition number and lose accuracy first. Rescaling time to [−1, 1] fixes every solver at once.

    How it's measured

    Polynomial trends with known coefficients are fitted over 250 trading days by the normal equations (Cholesky), Householder QR and a truncated SVD. The chart shows the relative error in the recovered coefficients. Gaps mean the solver broke down.

    Does a fitted price trend predict prices?

    This is where the project started: a straight line through eight days of prices. Across 210 walk-forward setups and 239,904 forecasts, no trend model beats simply predicting tomorrow's price is today's.

    How it's measured

    Seven series (MUTHOOTFIN, NIFTY 50, RELIANCE, TCS, HDFCBANK, INFY, ITC) are forecast with rolling windows of 8 to 250 days, polynomial degrees 0 to 2 and horizons of 1 and 5 days. Each setup is compared with the random walk by a Diebold-Mariano test. Rolling forecasts are a fixed linear filter derived once from a Householder QR.

    Market structure

    Price paths don't forecast, but how stocks move together has a clear shape. Random matrix theory separates that shape from noise.

    Is there structure beyond noise?

    One eigenvalue, the market mode, explains 28.4% of all variance and moves with the NIFTY 50 almost exactly. The next few are sector bets. Everything else sits in the band pure noise would produce.

    How it's measured

    PCA comes from the SVD of the standardised returns matrix (1,235 days × 51 stocks), never forming the correlation matrix explicitly. Independent series would put every eigenvalue inside the Marchenko-Pastur interval [(1 − √q)², (1 + √q)²]. Shuffling each stock's returns in time, which keeps fat tails but destroys co-movement, gives a largest eigenvalue of 1.45, matching the analytic edge.

    Can sectors be found without labels?

    Treat stocks as nodes of a graph linked by correlation and cut it along its weakest seams. With the market mode projected out, the cuts land almost exactly on NSE's own sectors. Leave it in and there's only one cluster: the market.

    How it's measured

    The low eigenvectors of the normalised Laplacian L = I − D⁻¹ᐟ²WD⁻¹ᐟ² embed the stocks and k-means groups them; the number of clusters comes from the largest eigengap. Sector labels are used only to score the result with the adjusted Rand index, against 2,000 random relabellings.

      Portfolios

      Three practical uses of that structure, each held out of sample: copying an index cheaply, lowering risk, and trading what the factors leave behind.

      Can ten stocks track the NIFTY 50?

      Baskets picked on 2021–2023 and held untouched through 2024–2025. A greedily chosen ten-stock basket tracks the index with 3.9% annual error, better than every one of 200 random ten-stock baskets.

      How it's measured

      Weights solve min ‖r_index − Rw‖² over long-only, fully invested portfolios, a quadratic program on the probability simplex solved exactly by an active-set method. An L1 penalty can't make that sparse (‖w‖₁ is always 1), so stocks are selected first, either by the order they enter a LASSO path or greedily, and then weighted. Random baskets get the same weighting, so the comparison isolates selection.

      Does cleaning the covariance matrix lower risk?

      A minimum-variance optimiser hunts for the directions where the sample covariance underestimates risk, so it buys noise. With little data, cleaning the estimate cuts realised volatility by over a quarter. With two years of data, or a no-shorting rule, the gain is gone.

      How it's measured

      Four estimators feed the same optimiser: the sample covariance, Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage, RMT eigenvalue clipping and a PCA factor model. Weights come from the KKT system via one Cholesky factorisation (or the simplex QP when long-only), rebalanced every 21 days and scored on 2023-01-13 to 2025-12-31. Differences use a Diebold-Mariano test on squared daily returns.

      Is there a stat-arb edge after costs?

      Strip each stock of its factor exposure and bet that what's left reverts. On US stocks in 1997–2007 this made money. Here it roughly breaks even before costs and loses steadily after them.

      How it's measured

      This is Avellaneda and Lee (2010) with the paper's own parameters, so nothing is tuned on this data. Residuals of a regression on the eigenportfolios are fitted as an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process; positions open when the s-score passes 1.25 and close at 0.75 or 0.5, each hedged with its factor exposure. Costs are charged one-way on every unit traded, stock legs and hedges alike.

      Time series

      Back to the original question with better tools: a trend that's allowed to change, and a market that's allowed to switch moods.

      Does a time-varying trend forecast better?

      A Kalman filter lets the trend's level and slope drift. The data prefers a slope that barely moves, and forecasts land within 1% of the random walk on all seven series. Forcing the trend to chase recent prices only makes the estimate noisier.

      How it's measured

      A local linear trend model is fitted by maximum likelihood, with the slope's signal-to-noise ratio profiled over a grid from 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻². A likelihood-ratio test rejects every ratio of 10⁻⁴ or more on all seven series. Forecasts are fitted on 2021–2022 and filtered causally through 2023–2025.

      Does the market switch between regimes?

      A hidden Markov model finds two: a calm regime most of the time and a turbulent one in short bursts. In the turbulent regime stocks move together far more, which is exactly when diversification is needed most. As a volatility forecaster, though, it only ties with a simple moving average.

      How it's measured

      A Gaussian HMM of daily NIFTY 50 returns is fitted with Baum-Welch using scaled forward-backward recursions; BIC picks two states over one or three. The lower panel is the market mode's share of a rolling 63-day correlation matrix. Forecasts use only filtered probabilities, so nothing looks ahead.

      The library underneath

      Every factorisation, eigensolver and optimiser is written by hand and tested against NumPy, SciPy or scikit-learn. NumPy does the array arithmetic; SciPy is used only for distributions and one likelihood maximisation.
